The Problem

What did US copyright law actually say in 1985?

  • Copyright law has been amended 121 times since 1790
  • Courts need the statute text at the time of infringement
  • No single source reconstructs the historical text
  • Lawyers pay hundreds of dollars for piecemeal historical research

The Solution

Treat legislation like source code.

Each Act of Congress = one git commit.

Bugs in the Law

When you apply every amendment mechanically, you find places where
Congress's own instructions don't compile.

§105 — Duplicate Subsection Letters

PL 116-92 (2019) created two subsections both lettered (c) — one for “Use by Federal Government,” one for “Definitions.” The duplicate persisted in law for three years until PL 117-263 fixed it.

§105 — Two Subparagraph (M)s

Same Public Law, two committees each added a different institution as subparagraph (M). Armed Services added the Merchant Marine Academy; Intelligence added National Intelligence University. Neither knew about the other.

§802 — History Erased

In 2004, Congress rewrote all of Chapter 8. The OLRC’s notes for the new §802 only cover post-2004 changes. 28 years of prior amendments are unrecoverable from current official sources. A legislative git push --force.

9 anomalies found so far. Version control doesn't just record the law — it audits it.
